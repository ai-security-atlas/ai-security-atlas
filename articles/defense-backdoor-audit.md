Backdoor-oriented artifact evaluation inspects a model or checkpoint specifically for conditional, trigger-activated behavior rather than only measuring average-case quality. It presumes familiarity with how [trigger-conditioned model backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md) are constructed, and it works by probing for divergence between clean utility and behavior under suspected trigger conditions — testing candidate triggers, comparing outputs across input variations, and scrutinizing artifacts whose provenance is uncertain.

As a mitigation it is conditional and inherently incomplete. It can raise the odds of catching [trigger-conditioned model backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md), [persistent conditional LLM backdoors](../nodes/attack-sleeper.md), and [diffusion backdoors / BadDiffusion](../nodes/attack-diffusion-backdoor.md), but the search space of possible triggers is effectively unbounded. Not finding a trigger is evidence of a limited search, not proof that none exists, and cited work shows that some conditional behaviors can survive downstream safety training and evade casual inspection. Adaptive adversaries can choose rare or composite triggers designed to slip past the tests actually run.

Because of this, evaluation is best treated as one layer alongside provenance and supply-chain controls that reduce how much untrusted artifacts are trusted in the first place. Refer to the cited studies for concrete detection methods and their measured limits.
