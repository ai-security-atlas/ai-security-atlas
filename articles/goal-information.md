This objective groups attacks whose goal is disclosure: inferring or extracting information the system was supposed to protect. That target can be training data, membership or attributes of individuals, a hidden system prompt, retrieved records, another user's session, or downstream secrets — anything the attacker is not authorized to see but the model or its context touches.

The techniques that route here differ by *where* the protected information lives. Against the model's learned parameters, [membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md), [model inversion and attribute inference](../nodes/attack-attribute.md), [language-model training-data extraction](../nodes/attack-training-extraction.md) and diffusion training-image extraction pull out what was memorized. Against the running context and deployment, [system or developer prompt disclosure](../nodes/attack-prompt-leak.md), [cross-session or cross-tenant disclosure](../nodes/attack-session-leak.md), [prompt-cache timing inference](../nodes/attack-cache-sidechannel.md) and reconstruction from gradients or updates leak live or side-channel data. In retrieval and agent settings, [unauthorized retrieved-document disclosure](../nodes/attack-rag-disclosure.md), [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md), confused-deputy abuse, tool-description poisoning and tool-output injection coax the system into revealing what it can access.

What unifies them is that the payoff is *knowledge* — reading something out — rather than changing an output or taking an action. Think of this objective as "what could an attacker learn that they shouldn't." For the conditions each disclosure route requires, see the individual techniques.
