Transformers are attention-based building blocks: stacks of self-attention and feed-forward layers that let each position in a sequence attend to others, learning context-dependent representations. Built on [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), the same block design is reused across many objectives and modalities rather than tied to one task. That generality is why the atlas places Transformers upstream of [Decoder-only autoregressive language models](../nodes/arch-decoder.md), [Encoder-only language models](../nodes/arch-encoder.md), [Encoder-decoder models](../nodes/arch-encdec.md), [Diffusion transformers](../nodes/arch-dit.md), and [Vision-language models](../nodes/arch-vlm.md).

## Why it matters for security analysis

A transformer backbone alone does not fix a training objective or generation procedure, so security properties should be read from the concrete model that uses it, not assumed from the block type. An encoder that classifies text and a decoder that generates it share attention layers but differ in threat surface. The atlas notes [adversarial activation steering](../nodes/attack-steering-misuse.md) as relevant to this scope, reflecting that transformer internals such as residual activations are a locus for interpretability-driven manipulation; this marks applicability, not a demonstrated weakness. For techniques tied to autoregressive generation, bidirectional encoding, or multimodal inputs, consult the specific downstream architecture. Empirical behavior and design details are deferred to the cited literature.
