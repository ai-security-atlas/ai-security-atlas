Variational autoencoders (VAEs) pair an encoder that maps inputs to a distribution over a latent space with a decoder that reconstructs data from latent samples. Built on [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), they are trained to reconstruct their inputs while regularizing the latent distribution, which yields a structured, continuous latent representation that can be sampled to generate new data. Beyond standalone generation, VAEs frequently serve as components within larger pipelines: the atlas records them as a prerequisite for [latent diffusion pipelines](../nodes/arch-latent-diffusion.md), where the VAE supplies the latent space, and for [latent spaces and codecs](../nodes/concept-latents.md).

## Why it matters for security analysis

Because a VAE may be an end-to-end generator or an embedded encoder-decoder, analysis should first fix its role in the system: what it encodes, what it decodes, and which downstream component consumes its latents. A concern affecting the encoder's representation is not the same as one affecting a full generative pipeline that merely uses the VAE as a codec. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own, so applicable scope is reasoned from the deployment and from model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), rather than assumed from other generative families such as GANs or diffusion. Design and empirical specifics are deferred to the cited literature.
