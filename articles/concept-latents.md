A *latent space* is a compressed internal representation in which a generative model does much of its work. Rather than operating directly on high-dimensional data such as raw pixels, many systems use an encoder to map inputs into a lower-dimensional latent, run generation there, and then use a decoder (together a *codec*) to reconstruct the full-resolution output. Working in the latent makes generation cheaper and often more stable. This concept builds on [variational autoencoders](../nodes/arch-vae.md), which provide the encoder–decoder pair that defines the latent.

The reason to name latents, encoders, and decoders separately is that each is its own component and therefore its own surface. The generative model that operates in latent space, the encoder that maps into it, and the decoder that maps back out are distinct pieces with distinct behaviors; a property of one does not automatically hold for the others. Treating "the model" as a single monolith obscures these seams.

For a security engineer, this decomposition is the practical point: a generative system is a pipeline of parts, and reasoning about it means locating which part a concern actually touches. How specific interventions engage the latent, encoder, or decoder is left to the techniques that build on this concept, with empirical detail deferred to the cited sources.
