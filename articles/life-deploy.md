Deployment, monitoring and updates is the lifecycle stage where a trained checkpoint becomes a running service: it is packaged, served, integrated with surrounding systems, observed in operation and periodically updated. The purpose is to make model capabilities available to users and applications while keeping the deployment observable and maintainable. It requires clear [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md), since serving a model means deciding who may call it, with what privileges, and across which interfaces.

A central point is that packaging, serving and integration change security exposure beyond the model checkpoint itself. The weights may be unchanged, yet the surrounding [inference engines and serving gateways](../nodes/system-serving.md), [model loaders and executable repository code](../nodes/system-loader.md), and [model registries and distribution](../nodes/system-registry.md) each add surface that the training stages did not have. Exposure here is a property of the deployment architecture — how requests are authenticated, how artifacts are loaded, and how updates are distributed — rather than of the model in isolation.

This distinguishes deployment from the preceding training and evaluation stages, whose concerns are about how the model was built; here the concern is how it is operated. The cited NIST taxonomy and OWASP application guidance frame operational exposures; defer to them for specific control recommendations.
