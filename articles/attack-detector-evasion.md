Evasion of an ML security detector modifies an input so that a specific classifier or deployed detector fails to flag it. Its objective is to [evade security controls or accountability](../nodes/goal-evasion.md): the underlying content is unchanged in purpose, but its representation is altered to slip past a particular model's decision boundary.

Conceptually the technique works against [classical predictive ML](../nodes/arch-classical.md) and [neural network](../nodes/arch-neural.md) detectors by finding an input region the detector scores as benign. The attacker-controlled input is the modification applied to the sample; the victim-controlled state is the deployed detector and its learned boundary. Access assumptions matter and should be stated: some evasions require only observing decisions, while others assume knowledge of the model or a surrogate.

A crucial scoping rule is that both the detector and the evasion metric must be specified. Success against one classifier under one metric does not generalize; in particular, results from a generic image-domain experiment should not be read as operational malware-detector evasion.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node here, but the mechanism points toward robust detector design and, importantly, [adaptive defense evaluation](../nodes/eval-adaptive.md) — testing a detector against an attacker who adapts to the defense, rather than against a fixed attack. Evaluation is also framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack belongs to the adversarial-input-evasion family. For measured evasion rates against specific detectors, defer to the cited sources.
