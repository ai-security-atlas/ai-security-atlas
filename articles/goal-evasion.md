This objective covers attacks aimed at a *control* rather than at the model's core task: defeating a deployed detector, monitoring pipeline, grader or attribution mechanism so that malicious activity goes unflagged or unattributed. The attacker's win is measured against that control's own metric — a sample that slips past a classifier, an artifact a judge waves through, a trail that resists tracing back.

The techniques that route here are organized around whatever the control measures. [Evasion of an ML security detector](../nodes/attack-detector-evasion.md) targets classifiers such as malware or abuse detectors directly. Perturbation methods — [gradient-based adversarial perturbations / FGSM](../nodes/attack-fgsm.md), [iterative projected optimization](../nodes/attack-pgd.md) and [physical-world adversarial examples](../nodes/attack-physical.md) — craft inputs a detector scores as benign. Against evaluation and oversight layers, [automated judge or grader manipulation](../nodes/attack-judge-manipulation.md), [benchmark or evaluation contamination](../nodes/attack-eval-contamination.md) and [evidence and citation laundering](../nodes/attack-citation-launder.md) corrupt how a system is scored or how claims are sourced.

The important distinction is that evading a control is metric-specific and is *not* identical to bypassing a model's own refusal behavior: one defeats an external gatekeeper, the other pushes the model itself past a rule. The two often appear together but are measured differently. For the specifics of each detector and grader attack, follow the techniques above.
