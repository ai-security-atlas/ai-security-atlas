Responsible disclosure and safe research practices govern how vulnerabilities and harmful capabilities in AI systems are investigated and reported: using authorized test environments, minimizing exposure of sensitive data during testing, and preserving workflows for correction and retraction. It presupposes respected [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md) — research is conducted with permission and within scope — so that security work does not itself become an uncontrolled release of dangerous artifacts or data.

As a control this is procedural and conditional. It can reduce the likelihood that research contributes to [AI-assisted harmful activity](../nodes/attack-capability-abuse.md) by keeping findings, exploit details, and sensitive material contained until fixes exist and by enabling material to be corrected or withdrawn. It does not remove the underlying capability or prevent independent misuse: disclosure norms bind only those who follow them, determined actors are unconstrained, and the balance between transparency and restraint is a judgment call with residual risk in either direction.

Because it addresses process rather than a technical attack surface, its value depends on institutional adoption and enforcement rather than on any mechanism in the system itself. The cited taxonomy and threat knowledge base provide the vocabulary and framing that structured disclosure programs build on.
