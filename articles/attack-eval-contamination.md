Benchmark or evaluation contamination influences a measurement by leaking evaluation material into training, or by altering the evaluation set itself. It routes toward [evading security controls or accountability](../nodes/goal-evasion.md) and [manipulating outputs, predictions or decisions](../nodes/goal-decisions.md), because a compromised measurement can make a system appear safer or more capable than it is.

Conceptually the technique targets [evaluation pipelines and automated judges](../nodes/system-eval.md) and the surrounding [evaluation data and benchmark governance](../nodes/life-evaluate.md). The attacker-controlled input is the leaked or altered evaluation content; the victim-controlled state is the training corpus and the benchmark's integrity, which downstream consumers trust as an honest yardstick. Where test items reach training, later scores may reflect memorization rather than capability.

A learner should keep three phenomena distinct, since the caveat stresses they are separate: benign overlap that arises incidentally, deliberate contamination introduced to inflate results, and manipulation of the grader itself. The last of these is a sibling technique, [automated judge or grader manipulation](../nodes/attack-judge-manipulation.md), which targets the scoring step rather than the data.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node here; the mechanism points toward benchmark governance, held-out and freshly sourced test data, and contamination auditing. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack belongs to the evaluation-and-accountability family. For documented contamination and its measured effect, defer to the cited sources.
