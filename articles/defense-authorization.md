Enforcing authorization outside the model means that the decision to permit an action is made in deterministic application code — checking the requesting principal, the target resource, and the requested action against a policy — rather than being delegated to the language model's discretion. It rests on well-defined [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md): the system must know who is acting and on whose behalf before any privileged operation runs, and that check must sit on the enforcement path where it cannot be talked out of by generated text.

This control conditionally reduces several failure modes. It can constrain [agent or connector confused-deputy abuse](../nodes/attack-confused-deputy.md) and [exploitation of excessive delegated agency](../nodes/attack-excessive-agency.md) by re-checking authority at the point of action instead of trusting the model's intent, and it can limit [unauthorized retrieved-document disclosure](../nodes/attack-rag-disclosure.md) when access rules are evaluated deterministically. It is a partial mitigation with residual risk: it only enforces the policy it is given, so overly broad grants, missing checks, or ambiguous principal identity still permit abuse, and it does nothing against attacks that operate within legitimately authorized scope.

It complements rather than replaces model-side measures, moving trust off the model and into code. See the cited authorization guidance for implementation considerations.
