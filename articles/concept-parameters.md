This concept draws a distinction that recurs throughout the atlas: *weights and parameters* versus *activations*. Parameters (weights) are the learned tensors fixed after training — the model's persistent knowledge, identical for every input and shared across all users. Activations are the intermediate values computed *during* a forward pass; they are input-dependent, transient, and different for every prompt. Put simply, weights are what the model *is*, activations are what it is *doing* right now. This concept builds on [neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), which supply the layered structure these tensors live in.

Keeping the two straight is essential because techniques target one or the other, and the difference determines persistence and access. Editing weights changes the model for everyone, permanently; manipulating activations affects only the current computation. Confusing them leads to wrong conclusions about what an intervention actually alters.

Because this distinction is foundational, a large share of the atlas depends on it. It is a prerequisite for [input and output embeddings](../nodes/concept-embeddings.md), [logits, probabilities and observable outputs](../nodes/concept-logits.md), [losses, gradients and optimization](../nodes/concept-gradients.md), [memorization, generalization and duplication](../nodes/concept-memorization.md), and [residual streams and block structure](../nodes/concept-residual.md), as well as parameter-level operations such as [quantization and numerical precision](../nodes/transform-quantization.md), [model and adapter merging](../nodes/transform-merging.md), and [unauthorized checkpoint or adapter theft](../nodes/attack-weight-theft.md).
