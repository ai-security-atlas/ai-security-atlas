Refusal-direction ablation, often called abliteration, describes a group of interventions that locate an internal direction associated with a model's refusal behavior and then suppress its influence. Research such as *Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction* reports that, in some models, refusal can be approximated by a single linear direction in the residual stream computed from the difference in mean activations between harmful and harmless instructions. The core mechanism is to subtract that unit direction $\hat{r}$ from residual-stream activations at inference time or to orthogonalize every residual-writing weight matrix against it ($W_{\text{out}}' \leftarrow W_{\text{out}} - \hat{r}\hat{r}^\top W_{\text{out}}$).

The technique builds on [representation engineering](../nodes/adapt-representation.md), which supplies the methods for finding behaviorally meaningful directions, and on an understanding of [residual streams and block structure](../nodes/concept-residual.md), where those directions are read and written.

As a scope note, ablating a refusal-related direction is narrower than removing every safeguard; other alignment behaviors, content filters and system-level controls may be unaffected. The same mechanism is relevant to security because it can be used by [adversarial refusal-direction modification](../nodes/attack-refusal-ablation.md), where the aim is to weaken guardrails rather than to study them. The empirical scope, including which models exhibit a clean single direction, is documented in the cited source.
