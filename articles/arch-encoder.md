Encoder-only language models produce bidirectional representations of an input: every position attends to the whole sequence at once, so the resulting embeddings capture context from both left and right. Built on [Transformers](../nodes/arch-transformer.md), they are typically pretrained with objectives such as masked-token prediction and then adapted to discriminative tasks like classification, tagging, or retrieval scoring. They are not generative in the autoregressive sense: rather than emitting a continuation token by token, they encode text into representations that downstream heads consume.

## Why it matters for security analysis

This distinction matters when scoping techniques. Threats framed around step-by-step text generation — prompt-based jailbreaks or refusal bypass, for instance — do not transfer automatically to a model whose role is to embed or classify rather than to produce free-form output. Security analysis of encoder-only models is better anchored in their actual use: how their representations feed decisions, how the fine-tuned task defines success and failure, and what an attacker gains by perturbing inputs or influencing training. Because this node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own, applicability should be reasoned from the deployed task and from broader model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), rather than assumed from superficially similar generative architectures. Design and empirical details are deferred to the cited literature.
