Transferability is the property that an attack crafted against one model or configuration also works against a *different* target the attacker did not directly optimize on. It is an appealing capability for an adversary, because it would let an attack developed against an accessible surrogate carry over to a target they cannot probe as freely. This concept builds on [neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), across which such carry-over is sometimes observed.

The disciplined framing this concept insists on is that transfer is an *empirical property to be measured, not assumed*. Success on one model or setting does not by itself establish success on another; targets differ in architecture, training, defenses, and configuration, any of which can blunt or block an attack that worked elsewhere. This mirrors the caution in [architectural applicability versus empirical evidence](../nodes/concept-adjacency.md): the mere plausibility of transfer is not a demonstration of it.

This concept is a prerequisite for [transfer of adversarial prompts](../nodes/attack-transfer-prompts.md), which specifically depends on inputs generalizing across models. For a security engineer assessing risk, the takeaway is to treat cross-model claims as hypotheses requiring evidence for the particular target in question, and to draw any reported transfer rates from the cited sources rather than generalizing from a single result.
