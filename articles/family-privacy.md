This family collects techniques that infer or reconstruct protected data, differing mainly in what the attacker can see and what they aim to recover. The unifying objective is *Obtain protected information*—training records, other users' data, or private attributes—without direct authorized access to it.

The members assume different visibility. [Membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md) asks only whether a specific record was in the training set, while [Model inversion and attribute inference](../nodes/attack-attribute.md) reconstructs features or attributes. [Language-model training-data extraction](../nodes/attack-training-extraction.md) and [Diffusion training-image extraction](../nodes/attack-diffusion-extraction.md) recover memorized content from generative models. [Reconstruction from gradients or updates](../nodes/attack-gradient-leak.md) targets the federated or distributed setting, [Prompt-cache timing inference](../nodes/attack-cache-sidechannel.md) exploits a side channel, and Cross-session or cross-tenant disclosure concerns data bleeding across isolation boundaries.

The atlas notes distinctions worth keeping straight. Training-data extraction is marked distinct from Unauthorized retrieved-document disclosure: the former recovers what a model memorized, the latter leaks documents a retrieval system surfaced at inference. Attribute inference is likewise distinguished from textual inversion. Use this grouping to navigate confidentiality threats by threat model; the boundary that matters is what the attacker observes and where the protected data resides.
