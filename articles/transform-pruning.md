Pruning and sparsity reduce a model's active parameters by removing or constraining weights and connections judged to contribute little to its output. The result is a sparser network that can be cheaper to store and, with suitable hardware or kernels, faster to run. A one-shot approach for large language models is described in *SparseGPT: Massive Language Models Can Be Accurately Pruned in One-Shot*.

The method operates on a model's [weights, parameters and activations](../nodes/concept-parameters.md), selecting which of them to drop or zero while trying to keep the retained network's behavior close to the original. Choosing what to prune, and how aggressively, is the core design question, since removing too much degrades quality.

Pruning is a neutral efficiency and compression technique, and within this dataset it carries no direct link to a specific attack. Its summary adds an important caution: security effects are not guaranteed by compression. Making a model smaller or sparser does not by itself remove undesired behavior or eliminate embedded risks, so any safety claim about a pruned model has to be evaluated on its own. How much sparsity a model tolerates before accuracy drops is left to the cited source.
