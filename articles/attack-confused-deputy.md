A confused-deputy abuse induces a more-privileged component to exercise its authority on behalf of an unauthorized requester. It routes toward [causing unauthorized actions or privilege use](../nodes/goal-actions.md) and [obtaining protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md): the deputy holds credentials or access the requester lacks, and is tricked into using them.

Conceptually the technique targets [tool-using agents](../nodes/system-agent.md) wired to [tools, plugins, MCP and connectors](../nodes/system-tools.md), and it turns on [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md). The attacker-controlled input is a request or content that the deputy will act upon; the victim-controlled state is the deputy's standing privileges and the assumption that anything reaching it is authorized. The core distinction to internalize is that authenticating a protocol connection is not the same as authorizing a specific semantic action — a component may verify who is calling yet still perform an operation the caller was never entitled to trigger.

This abuse may be enabled by [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md), where lower-trust content supplies the instruction the deputy then executes with its own authority.

Candidate mitigations reduce but do not eliminate risk: [enforcing authorization outside the model](../nodes/defense-authorization.md) so action approval does not depend on model judgment, and [least-privilege tools and scoped credentials](../nodes/defense-least-privilege.md) so a confused deputy can do less. Evaluation is framed through the [AgentDojo benchmark](../nodes/eval-agentdojo.md), [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), and an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). For observed cases, defer to the cited source.
