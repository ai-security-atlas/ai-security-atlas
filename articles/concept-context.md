The context window is the span of tokens a model can attend to at once — the prompt, prior turns, and any retrieved or tool-supplied text present during a single generation. *In-context learning* is the observation that a model can adapt its behavior from examples or instructions placed in that window, without any change to its weights. Give it a few worked examples and it often follows the pattern, purely from what is in front of it.

The key distinction this concept draws is between information *in the current context* and information *encoded in persistent weights*. Weights are fixed at inference time and shared across all users; context is transient, per-request, and frequently populated with untrusted material. Behavior can shift dramatically based on context alone. This concept requires [decoder-only autoregressive language models](../nodes/arch-decoder.md), whose left-to-right generation consumes the window as it produces each token.

Because context is both influential and often attacker-reachable, it is a prerequisite for a cluster of techniques: [direct prompt injection](../nodes/attack-direct-injection.md), [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md), [jailbreaking and refusal bypass](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md), [many-shot context attacks](../nodes/attack-many-shot.md), [system or developer prompt disclosure](../nodes/attack-prompt-leak.md), and [token, context and inference-budget exhaustion](../nodes/attack-token-exhaustion.md). Understanding what the window contains, and who controlled it, is foundational to reasoning about these.
