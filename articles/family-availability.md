This family collects techniques whose objective is to *Degrade availability or increase cost* rather than to alter what a model outputs. The shared mechanism is resource pressure: an attacker crafts inputs or interactions that consume disproportionate compute, memory, context, or budget relative to their size or apparent complexity.

Its members work at different layers. [Energy and latency amplification / sponge inputs](../nodes/attack-sponge.md) target the model's own inference cost, shaping inputs that maximize computation or latency. [Token, context and inference-budget exhaustion](../nodes/attack-token-exhaustion.md) drives up consumption of context windows and metered tokens. [Tool-loop and recursive-delegation exhaustion](../nodes/attack-agent-loop.md) exploits agentic control flow, provoking uncontrolled loops or cascading delegation that may not terminate cleanly.

What distinguishes this family from most others in the atlas is intent: the goal is denial or expense, not manipulated predictions, extracted data, or persistent influence. A technique that happens to be slow is not necessarily an availability attack; membership here reflects that cost or uptime is the payoff the attacker seeks. Use the grouping to navigate toward denial-of-service-style concerns in AI systems, keeping in mind that some members can accompany other attacks—an agent loop, for instance, may arise alongside injection—without changing where the primary harm lands.
