Automated judge or grader manipulation influences the grading instructions, context, or feedback of an evaluation so that a system appears more successful or safer than it is. It routes toward [evading security controls or accountability](../nodes/goal-evasion.md), targeting the measurement rather than the underlying behavior.

Conceptually the technique treats an automated grader as an attack surface. It depends on [evaluation pipelines and automated judges](../nodes/system-eval.md) and on [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md): where the material being judged can also influence the judge — for instance by carrying text the grader reads as instruction — the score can be steered. The attacker-controlled input is the content or context that reaches the judge; the victim-controlled state is the grading rubric and the judge model whose verdict is trusted downstream.

This is a coverage scenario rather than an established empirical result for every grader: whether a particular judge can be swayed needs judge-specific validation before any success claim is published. A learner should distinguish it from its sibling [benchmark or evaluation contamination](../nodes/attack-eval-contamination.md), which corrupts the data or test set rather than the scoring step itself.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node here; the mechanism points toward isolating judge instructions from graded content and validating judges adversarially. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack belongs to the evaluation-and-accountability family. For demonstrated manipulations, defer to the cited sources.
