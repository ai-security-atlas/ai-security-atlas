Reconstruction from gradients or updates recovers information about training inputs from exposed gradients or model updates. It routes toward [obtaining protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md): rather than querying a finished model, it reads the intermediate signals produced while the model learns.

Conceptually the technique depends on [losses, gradients and optimization](../nodes/concept-gradients.md). Because a gradient computed on a batch encodes how that specific data pushed the parameters, an observer can, under the right conditions, invert the relationship to approximate the underlying inputs for a [neural network](../nodes/arch-neural.md). The attacker-controlled input is the reconstruction procedure applied to observed updates; the victim-controlled state is the training data reflected in those updates and the setting — such as collaborative or federated training — that exposes them.

The decisive access condition, and the reason this is not a generic API attack, is that it requires visibility into gradients or updates. Ordinary text-in, text-out API access does not supply that visibility, so the technique presumes a materially different vantage point than most inference-time attacks.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node for this entry; the mechanism points toward not exposing raw updates and toward noise or aggregation defenses (as studied for private training more broadly). Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and [privacy verification and baselines](../nodes/eval-privacy.md). This attack belongs to the privacy-inference family. For the conditions under which reconstruction succeeds and its fidelity, defer to the cited source.
