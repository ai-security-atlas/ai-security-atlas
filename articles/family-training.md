This family groups techniques in which an attacker influences the data, objectives, or update process of training and adaptation, so that the resulting model behaves differently. The unifying assumption is participation in the learning loop—supplying fine-tuning data, providing preference signals, or contributing adapters—rather than manipulating a deployed model through its prompts.

Members act at distinct control points. [Adversarial fine-tuning to alter constraints](../nodes/attack-safety-finetune.md) uses further training to erode a model's safety behavior. [Preference or reward-signal manipulation](../nodes/attack-preference-poison.md) corrupts the feedback that alignment procedures optimize against. [Malicious adapters or adapter substitution](../nodes/attack-adapter-tamper.md) delivers altered behavior through lightweight adaptation modules attached to a base model.

These route toward *Violate intended behavioral constraints*, *Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions*, and *Establish persistent attacker influence*. The family overlaps with poisoning, which also corrupts learning inputs; the emphasis here is on the adaptation stage—fine-tuning, preference optimization, and adapters—where a model is customized after pretraining. It also borders the supply-chain family, since a substituted adapter is both an adaptation and an artifact concern. Treat the grouping as an orientation aid for threats that arise when customization is attacker-influenced.
