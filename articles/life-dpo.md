Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a post-training method that adjusts a model directly from preference comparisons, optimizing a preference-learning objective without training a separate reward model to score outputs. It requires [preference data and reward models](../nodes/life-preferences.md) as input — pairs of preferred and dispreferred responses — and uses them to shift the policy toward the preferred behavior.

A key distinction the atlas draws is between standard DPO and reward-model-plus-PPO training such as [RLHF and policy optimization](../nodes/life-rlhf.md). Both learn from human or synthetic preferences, but DPO folds the preference signal into a single objective rather than first fitting a reward model and then running reinforcement-learning policy updates against it. Which route was used matters when reasoning about how a given model's behavior was shaped.

Security-relevant scope follows from the dependency on preference data. Because the objective is defined by which comparisons are labeled preferred, the trustworthiness and sourcing of those comparisons are relevant to the resulting behavior — a concern shared with any preference-driven stage rather than a claim about any particular training run. The cited DPO paper provides the formal objective and its relationship to reward modeling; consult it for empirical results rather than inferring behavioral guarantees here.
