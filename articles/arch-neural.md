Neural networks are parameterized, differentiable models trained by gradient-based optimization. They are the shared substrate beneath most of the architectures in this atlas rather than a specific generative family: layers of weighted transformations and nonlinearities map inputs to outputs, and their parameters are fit to data by minimizing a loss. Because so much rests on this foundation, the atlas treats [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md) as a prerequisite for [Transformers](../nodes/arch-transformer.md), [Convolutional networks](../nodes/arch-cnn.md), [Variational autoencoders](../nodes/arch-vae.md), [Mixture-of-experts models](../nodes/arch-moe.md), [State-space and hybrid sequence models](../nodes/arch-ssm.md), and others.

## Why it matters for security analysis

Many technique families are defined at this level of generality because they exploit properties common to differentiable models rather than any one design. Relevant to this scope are gradient-based work such as [gradient-based adversarial perturbations / FGSM](../nodes/attack-fgsm.md) and [iterative projected adversarial optimization](../nodes/attack-pgd.md); data-facing concerns like [pretraining data poisoning](../nodes/attack-pretrain-poison.md), [membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md), and [model inversion and attribute inference](../nodes/attack-attribute.md); supply-chain issues such as [trigger-conditioned model backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md); and extraction concerns including [black-box functional model extraction](../nodes/attack-model-imitation.md) and [reconstruction from gradients or updates](../nodes/attack-gradient-leak.md). Listing a technique here marks applicability, not a demonstrated weakness; concrete conditions and results belong to each specific architecture and to the cited literature.
