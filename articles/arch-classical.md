Classical predictive machine learning covers classifiers, regressors, decision trees, and ensembles — models fit to labeled data to predict a label or value rather than to generate open-ended content. Though overshadowed in discussion by large language models, they remain an essential baseline: many production security and decision systems rely on them, and they anchor the comparison for what generative architectures add or change. They are also the foundation the atlas requires for an [evasion of an ML security detector](../nodes/attack-detector-evasion.md), where the target model is a classifier making a security judgment.

## Why it matters for security analysis

The relevant scope here is defined by prediction APIs and trained decision boundaries rather than by prompts or generation. Techniques marked relevant include [black-box functional model extraction](../nodes/attack-model-imitation.md), which reconstructs a model's behavior from query access; [membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md) and [model inversion and attribute inference](../nodes/attack-attribute.md), which probe what training data or attributes a model reveals; and [evasion of an ML security detector](../nodes/attack-detector-evasion.md), which crafts inputs to slip past a classifier. These indicate applicability, not proven weakness in any given deployment. Analysis should focus on the model's task, its exposed query interface, and the sensitivity of its training data and outputs. Concrete attack conditions and results are deferred to the cited literature.
