Risk-based human approval inserts a person into the loop before an AI system performs a high-impact action, so that consequential operations are gated on an informed human decision rather than on the model's own judgment. It presupposes a [tool-using agent](../nodes/system-agent.md) whose actions can be intercepted and surfaced for review, and it works by classifying actions by risk and routing the riskier ones — irreversible changes, data exfiltration, spending — to a human who sees enough context to consent meaningfully.

As a control it is conditional and partial. It can raise the cost of [exploitation of excessive delegated agency](../nodes/attack-excessive-agency.md) by denying the agent unattended authority over the most damaging steps, and it offers some leverage against [tool definition or behavior changes after approval](../nodes/attack-tool-rugpull.md) when approval is re-solicited on change rather than granted once. It does not prevent these attacks: approvals that are vague, batched, or fatiguing degrade into rubber-stamping, and a reviewer who cannot see the true arguments or destination may approve something other than what executes.

Approval is most effective bound to concrete action parameters and paired with scoping controls rather than presented as a universal safeguard. Consult the cited guidance for placement and failure modes.
