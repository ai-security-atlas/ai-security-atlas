Separating data from authority is the principle that lower-trust content pulled into a model's context — retrieved documents, tool output, user-supplied text — must not be able to grant itself privileges or be interpreted as trusted instructions. It depends on clear [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md): the system tracks which parts of the context are authoritative and which are merely data, and keeps the latter from steering privileged behavior.

This is a defense-in-depth measure, not a perfect filter. It can conditionally reduce the impact of [direct prompt injection](../nodes/attack-direct-injection.md) and [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md) by ensuring that instructions embedded in untrusted content do not automatically inherit the operator's authority. Because current models do not reliably distinguish instructions from data on their own, any purely prompt-level separation can be probed and eventually bypassed by a determined attacker, so the durable protection comes from enforcing the boundary in surrounding architecture — scoping what actions injected text could ever trigger — rather than from wording alone.

It pairs naturally with out-of-model authorization and least-privilege tooling, which contain the consequences when a boundary is crossed. Consult the cited work on real-world indirect injection for how these boundaries are attacked and reinforced.
