Auditable action and content lineage records what an agent did and why in a privacy-conscious way — capturing tool decisions, the provenance of content, and the sequence of actions so that incidents can be investigated after the fact. It applies to [tool-using agents](../nodes/system-agent.md), whose behavior is otherwise opaque, and it works by preserving a reviewable trail of decisions and data flow rather than attempting to extract or expose the model's hidden reasoning.

As a control it is conditional and largely detective rather than preventive. It supports response to [persistent agent-memory poisoning](../nodes/attack-memory-poison.md) and [tool-output injection](../nodes/attack-tool-output.md) by making it possible to trace when tainted content entered, what it caused, and which state needs revocation — but logging observes; it does not by itself stop these attacks in progress. Residual risk includes gaps in what is captured, logs that are tampered with or insufficiently protected, and the tension between useful detail and privacy, which can leave sensitive data over- or under-recorded.

Its value is realized only when logs are actually monitored and tied to remediation such as memory review or credential rotation. The cited taxonomy and agentic-application guidance describe the lineage and provenance signals worth capturing.
