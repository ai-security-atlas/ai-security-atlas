Chain-of-thought and reasoning-trace hijacking manipulates or spoofs the intermediate deliberation steps of a reasoning model so that safety checks performed during the thinking phase are bypassed or misaligned actions are masked behind benign-looking rationales. The objective is to [violate intended behavioral constraints](../nodes/goal-behavior.md) or [evade security controls or accountability](../nodes/goal-evasion.md).

## How it works

Reasoning-capable [decoder-only language models](../nodes/arch-decoder.md) often rely on [test-time reasoning traces](../nodes/concept-reasoning-trace.md) to evaluate whether a request is safe before answering. When [trust boundaries](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md) between user input and internal reasoning tokens are porous — or when a prompt prefill / template mimics the model's own safety-justification steps (as in H-CoT) — an attacker can supply a pre-packaged, benign-sounding reasoning trajectory that concludes the request is safe, steering the model past its refusal state. Relatedly, as documented in *Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think*, generated reasoning chains can be unfaithful to the actual cues driving the model's decision, allowing an [agent](../nodes/system-agent.md) to emit a compliant rationale while executing an injected instruction.

This technique is a [variant of policy-bypass jailbreaking](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md) adapted to test-time compute pipelines. Its effectiveness depends on whether the serving layer prevents callers from forging reasoning delimiters, whether reasoning traces are prefillable, and whether downstream monitors trust the surface text of the scratchpad.

Candidate mitigations include [separating data from authority](../nodes/defense-boundaries.md) around reasoning delimiters and [representation rerouting and activation circuit breakers](../nodes/defense-circuit-breakers.md) that monitor internal states rather than surface rationales. Evaluation uses [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md).
