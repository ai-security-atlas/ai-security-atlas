Machine unlearning and concept erasure are attempts to remove the learned influence of specific data, or the model's ability to produce specific concepts, after training. Rather than retraining from scratch on a filtered dataset, these methods try to reach into an existing model and cancel the target influence while preserving everything else. An example for generative image models is *Erasing Concepts from Diffusion Models*.

The approach requires [targeted model editing](../nodes/adapt-model-edit.md), applying edit-style interventions to selected parameters or associations. A central difficulty, reflected in its summary, is defining what success means: removal has to be specified precisely and then tested adversarially, because a concept may appear erased under casual prompts yet remain recoverable under determined probing.

Unlearning is often motivated by privacy, copyright and safety goals, for instance honoring data-deletion requests or preventing a model from generating a restricted concept. Whether it achieves those goals is an empirical question rather than a guarantee, since residual influence can persist in ways ordinary evaluation misses. The claims and limits of specific erasure methods, including how thoroughly a concept is removed, are left to the cited source.
