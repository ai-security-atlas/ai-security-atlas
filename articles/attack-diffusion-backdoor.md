Diffusion backdoors alter a diffusion model's training or sampling process so that a trigger elicits an attacker-chosen outcome while ordinary generations look normal. It is a [variant of trigger-conditioned model backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md), specialized to generative image models, and routes toward [establishing persistent attacker influence](../nodes/goal-persistence.md) and [manipulating outputs, predictions or decisions](../nodes/goal-decisions.md).

Conceptually the technique intervenes in the [noise schedules and denoising objectives](../nodes/concept-noise.md) that define how a [diffusion generative model](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) transforms noise into samples, binding a trigger to a target output during that process. The attacker-controlled input is the poisoned training signal and the chosen trigger; the victim-controlled state is the model and the sampler that later runs it. Because the implant is tied to the denoising dynamics, it activates only under the trigger condition and otherwise preserves apparent utility.

What separates this from the general backdoor concept is that its assumptions are sampler- and process-specific: a construction demonstrated for one formulation does not automatically transfer to every flow-matching or transformer-based diffusion model. A learner should treat the process assumptions as part of the claim.

The candidate mitigation is [backdoor-oriented artifact evaluation](../nodes/defense-backdoor-audit.md), which probes for triggered behavior but cannot certify the absence of an unknown trigger. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack sits in the poisoning-and-persistence family. For the specific triggers and conditions studied, defer to the cited source.
