Activation steering changes a model's runtime behavior by adding a chosen intervention to its internal activations as it processes input. A steering vector, derived to represent some trait or direction, is injected at one or more layers so the model's generation shifts toward or away from that direction, without any change to the stored weights. The approach is described in *Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering*.

The method requires [representation engineering](../nodes/adapt-representation.md), which supplies the means to find meaningful directions, and an understanding of [residual streams and block structure](../nodes/concept-residual.md), where the intervention is applied. It also requires appropriate activation access, meaning the ability to read and write internal states at inference time.

Steering is a neutral capability used for controllable generation, interpretability and safety research, since the same handle that suppresses an unwanted trait can amplify a desired one. That symmetry is why it is relevant to security: it can be used by [adversarial activation steering](../nodes/attack-steering-misuse.md), where the intervention is chosen to push the model toward disallowed behavior. Whether a given use is benign depends on the intervention and intent, not the technique. Specific vector-derivation methods are left to the cited source.
