This family covers a single but important case: using a legitimate model capability for harmful ends without assuming the model is compromised. The distinguishing premise is that nothing about the model needs to be broken, poisoned, or jailbroken in the classic sense—the harm comes from applying a working capability to an abusive task.

Its member is [AI-assisted harmful activity](../nodes/attack-capability-abuse.md), which routes toward the objective *Use AI to amplify harmful activity*. In practice this describes an actor leveraging generation, analysis, or automation to scale work that would otherwise be slower or require more expertise.

What sets this family apart from the rest of the atlas is where the fault lies. Most families describe an attacker subverting a system's intended behavior—through injected instructions, corrupted training data, tampered weights, or stolen artifacts. Capability misuse instead concerns intended behavior being turned to a harmful purpose, which places much of the mitigation burden on usage policy, access controls, and monitoring rather than on model robustness alone. Treat this grouping as an orientation label for that threat model; it is deliberately narrow, and it exists to distinguish "the model worked as designed, misused" from "the model was made to misbehave."
