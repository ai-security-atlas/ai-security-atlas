Privacy verification and baselines concerns how a privacy attack's success is measured credibly. The essentials are a membership baseline (what an adversary could guess without the attack), record-level verification that a claimed leak really corresponds to a training or protected record, a confidence notion for each claim, and a similarity criterion appropriate to the data and task. Without these, apparent leakage can be indistinguishable from chance or from generic, non-memorized content.

## What it evaluates

This measure applies across disclosure and inference attacks: [membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md), [language-model training-data extraction](../nodes/attack-training-extraction.md), [diffusion training-image extraction](../nodes/attack-diffusion-extraction.md), [model inversion and attribute inference](../nodes/attack-attribute.md), [reconstruction from gradients or updates](../nodes/attack-gradient-leak.md), [cross-session or cross-tenant disclosure](../nodes/attack-session-leak.md), [prompt-cache timing inference](../nodes/attack-cache-sidechannel.md), and [system or developer prompt disclosure](../nodes/attack-prompt-leak.md).

A common pitfall is reporting raw success without a baseline, so that results above random guessing look dramatic when they are marginal, or counting near-duplicate generic text as a memorized record. Privacy results are scope-limited: they hold for the tested records, population, and similarity threshold, and a demonstrated leak is not proof that all data is exposed, nor is a null result proof of privacy. Verification depth and the chosen baseline heavily shape the conclusion. Defer to the cited sources for baseline constructions, verification procedures, and reported measurements.
