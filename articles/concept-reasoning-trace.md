Test-time reasoning traces are intermediate autoregressive sequences — often delimited as `<think>` or scratchpad blocks — that a [decoder-only language model](../nodes/arch-decoder.md) generates before emitting its final response. Rather than relying solely on a single forward pass per output token, reasoning-trained models use [decoding and sampling configuration](../nodes/concept-decoding.md) and extended [context windows](../nodes/concept-context.md) to spend variable inference compute decomposing a task, verifying candidate steps, or checking policy constraints.

Because the reasoning trace sits inside the model's active context window, every token generated during deliberation conditions the final answer and any subsequent tool calls. Some deployments expose the full trace to the caller, while others summarize or hide the raw thinking tokens and return only the final completion.

For security analysis, test-time reasoning introduces both a cost surface and an integrity surface. Because the length of the internal trace is determined dynamically by the input, adversaries can trigger [reasoning-token exhaustion and overthinking attacks](../nodes/attack-reasoning-sponge.md) that inflate inference compute without lengthening the visible prompt. At the same time, safety mechanisms that depend on self-correction inside the trace are exposed to [chain-of-thought and reasoning-trace hijacking](../nodes/attack-cot-hijack.md), and a readable trace is not guaranteed to be a faithful explanation of the internal factors driving the model's output.
