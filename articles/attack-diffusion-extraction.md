Diffusion training-image extraction recovers or closely reproduces images from a model's training set, under the specific conditions studied in extraction research. It routes toward [obtaining protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md), treating the model's generations as a channel back to data it saw during training.

Conceptually the technique depends on [diffusion generative models](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) and on [memorization, generalization and duplication](../nodes/concept-memorization.md): images that were duplicated or otherwise memorized are the ones most susceptible to being regenerated. The attacker-controlled input is the sampling and search procedure used to elicit and identify candidate matches; the victim-controlled state is what the model has internalized, which is heavily influenced by how often an image appeared. Extraction is therefore a property of memorization, not a guarantee for arbitrary training images.

Claims here require careful scoping: the similarity threshold, the reference corpus, and the deduplication procedure all determine what counts as a successful extraction. A study reported training-image extraction under its own evaluation conditions on the diffusion models it examined, while noting no universal success rate or applicability to all diffusion and flow models — so intuition should not be substituted for that evidence.

The candidate mitigation is [training-data minimization and deduplication](../nodes/defense-data-hygiene.md), which reduces the memorization that extraction exploits without eliminating it. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and [privacy verification and baselines](../nodes/eval-privacy.md). For the measured outcomes and thresholds, defer to the cited source.
