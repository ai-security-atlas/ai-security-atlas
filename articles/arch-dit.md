Diffusion transformers (DiT) use a [Transformers](../nodes/arch-transformer.md) backbone as the denoising network inside a [diffusion generative model](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md). They replace the convolutional denoiser common in earlier image diffusion with transformer blocks operating over patch or token representations, which improves scalability. The atlas records both prerequisites: DiT requires diffusion and transformers, and it uses the transformer block as its computational core.

## Why it matters for security analysis

The key distinction for analysis is that sharing a transformer backbone does not make a DiT an autoregressive language model. Its training objective is denoising and its generation is iterative diffusion sampling, so techniques scoped to token-by-token text generation — prompt injection, refusal bypass, adversarial suffixes — should not be assumed to transfer merely because attention layers are present. The relevant scope is better inherited from the diffusion family and from the concrete pipeline the DiT sits in, such as whether it operates in pixel or latent space and how conditioning is supplied. Distinguish it from [U-Net-style diffusion backbones](../nodes/arch-unet.md), which realize the same denoising role with convolutional networks, and from language-oriented transformer models. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own; applicability should be reasoned through the diffusion process it implements, with empirical detail deferred to the cited literature.
