Prompt-cache timing inference uses observable differences in service response timing to investigate whether a caching layer is shared across users or whether a particular prompt was seen before. Its objective is to [obtain protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md) — not by reading data directly, but by treating latency as a signal.

Conceptually the technique depends on [KV caches and prefix reuse](../nodes/concept-kv-cache.md): when a serving system reuses cached computation for a matching prefix, a cache hit may complete faster than a miss. The attacker-controlled input is a set of crafted prompts and timing measurements; the victim-controlled state is the [inference engine or serving gateway](../nodes/system-serving.md) and its caching policy. Where a cache is shared across tenants, timing may reveal something about other users' recent prompts.

Two conditions must be demonstrated rather than assumed: that the cache is actually shared in the relevant scope, and that the timing difference yields a statistically usable signal above measurement noise. Absent either, the channel is theoretical.

The candidate mitigation is [cache and session isolation](../nodes/defense-cache-isolation.md), which scopes reuse so that one user's prompts cannot influence another's timing; this narrows but does not automatically close every timing side channel. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and [privacy verification and baselines](../nodes/eval-privacy.md). This attack belongs to the privacy-inference family. For measured signal strength under specific serving setups, defer to the cited source.
