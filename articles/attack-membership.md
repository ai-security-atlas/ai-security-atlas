Membership inference estimates whether a particular candidate record was included in a model's training set. It routes toward [obtaining protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md): even without recovering data, learning that a specific record was used can itself be sensitive.

Conceptually the technique compares how a model behaves on a candidate versus how it behaves on data it never saw, reading signals from [logits, probabilities and observable outputs](../nodes/concept-logits.md). It relies on [memorization, generalization and duplication](../nodes/concept-memorization.md): models often respond with slightly more confidence to examples they trained on. The attacker-controlled input is the candidate record and any reference or shadow data used for calibration; the victim-controlled state is the [classical predictive ML](../nodes/arch-classical.md) or [neural network](../nodes/arch-neural.md) model under test.

A necessary framing is that the attack needs a meaningful candidate and a proper baseline — a claim of membership is only interpretable against a comparison of what non-members look like. Membership inference is also narrower than full-record reconstruction: it answers an in-or-out question, not what the record contained.

The candidate mitigation is [differentially private training](../nodes/defense-dp.md), which bounds any single record's influence and thereby limits the confidence gap, at a possible cost to utility; it reduces rather than removes signal. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and [privacy verification and baselines](../nodes/eval-privacy.md). For measured accuracy relative to baseline, defer to the cited source.
