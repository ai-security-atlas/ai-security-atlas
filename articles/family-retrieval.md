This family groups techniques that influence the external evidence a system retrieves, how that evidence is selected or ranked, and how it is authorized or attributed. The shared assumption is that many AI systems ground their answers in retrieved documents, so an attacker who can shape what gets retrieved—or how it is cited—may shape the output without touching the model.

Members act at different points in the pipeline. [RAG knowledge-base poisoning](../nodes/attack-rag-poison.md) plants adversarial content in the corpus a system draws on. [Retrieval and ranking manipulation](../nodes/attack-retrieval-rank.md) targets selection, promoting attacker-chosen passages. [Evidence and citation laundering](../nodes/attack-citation-launder.md) exploits attribution to lend false authority to a claim, and [Unauthorized retrieved-document disclosure](../nodes/attack-rag-disclosure.md) leaks documents the retrieval layer should not have surfaced.

These route toward *Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions*, *Evade security controls or accountability*, and *Obtain protected information*. The atlas distinguishes retrieved-document disclosure from Language-model training-data extraction: here the data leaks from a retrieval index at inference time, not from what the model memorized during training. Use this grouping to reason about the knowledge and grounding layer as its own attack surface, separate from the model weights and from the prompt channel.
