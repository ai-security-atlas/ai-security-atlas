Gradient-guided adversarial suffixes optimize a discrete string, appended to a prompt, using gradient information from the target model or a surrogate. It is a [variant of jailbreaking and refusal bypass](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md) and routes toward [violating intended behavioral constraints](../nodes/goal-behavior.md), searching for a suffix that shifts the model toward a disallowed response.

Conceptually the technique combines three prerequisites: the objective of [jailbreaking](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md), the machinery of [losses, gradients and optimization](../nodes/concept-gradients.md), and the model's [tokenization and vocabulary](../nodes/concept-tokens.md), since the search operates over discrete tokens rather than continuous pixels. The attacker-controlled input is the optimized suffix; the victim-controlled state is the aligned [language model](../nodes/arch-decoder.md) whose gradients or a surrogate's gradients guide the search. A load-bearing distinction is that this is a white-box or surrogate-driven construction and should not be mislabeled as purely black-box discovery — the optimization needs gradient access somewhere in the loop.

Because a suffix found this way can sometimes carry over to other models, it is required by the study of [transfer of adversarial prompts](../nodes/attack-transfer-prompts.md), which examines that cross-model behavior.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node for this entry; refusal-robustness measures discussed under jailbreaking are the natural reference. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and the [HarmBench benchmark](../nodes/eval-harmbench.md). For measured and transfer success rates, defer to the cited source.
