A sampler is the algorithm that actually runs a diffusion or flow model's reverse process at inference time. Having learned to denoise, the model still needs a procedure to step from noise to a finished sample: the sampler defines the *schedule* of timesteps and the *solver* — the numerical method that integrates the trajectory step by step. Different samplers can trade fewer steps for speed against more steps for fidelity, and different solvers follow the path with different accuracy. This concept builds on [noise schedules and denoising objectives](../nodes/concept-noise.md), which set up the process the sampler traverses.

The defining property is that samplers *affect cost and outcomes independently of the weights*. Two runs of the identical trained model can differ in latency and in the details of what they produce purely because of the sampler chosen. In that sense a sampler is to a diffusion model what decoding configuration is to a language model: an inference-time knob, not a change to what the model has learned.

For a security engineer, that separation is the useful part — inference behavior and trained behavior are distinct levers, and questions about one should not be attributed to the other. Concrete comparisons between samplers, including efficiency and quality effects, belong to the cited sources rather than to assumption.
