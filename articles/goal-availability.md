This objective concerns attacks that make an AI system slower, more expensive or unavailable, rather than making it say or do something specific. An attacker pursuing it may want to exhaust a token budget, drive up inference cost, saturate tool or compute capacity, or push latency and energy use to a point where the service degrades for everyone.

The techniques that route here share a focus on *resource asymmetry*: crafting inputs or interaction patterns where a small amount of attacker effort forces a disproportionate amount of work from the system. [Energy and latency amplification / sponge inputs](../nodes/attack-sponge.md) target the model's per-request compute and power draw. [Token, context and inference-budget exhaustion](../nodes/attack-token-exhaustion.md) inflates the tokens or context a request consumes. [Tool-loop and recursive-delegation exhaustion](../nodes/attack-agent-loop.md) turns an agent's own planning and delegation against it, spinning up runaway chains of calls.

A useful distinction for this objective is to separate genuinely algorithmic effects — where the *content* of an input triggers pathological cost — from generic request floods that any network service faces and that are better handled as ordinary denial-of-service. This partition is about the former. For the specific input shapes and loop conditions involved, consult the individual techniques above.
