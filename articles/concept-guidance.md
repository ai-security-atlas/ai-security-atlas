Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is an inference-time technique in diffusion generation that strengthens how closely an output follows its conditioning. At each denoising step the model produces two predictions — one conditioned on the prompt and one unconditioned — and CFG extrapolates away from the unconditioned prediction toward the conditioned one, scaled by a guidance weight. Higher weights push the sample to adhere more tightly to the prompt; lower weights allow more freedom. It builds on [diffusion generative models](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) and [text, image and control conditioning](../nodes/concept-conditioning.md), and the "classifier-free" name signals that it achieves this *without* a separate trained classifier guiding the process.

The important clarification is what CFG is *not*. Despite the word "classifier," it is a **generative** guidance mechanism concerned with sample fidelity to the conditioning — not a safety or content-policy classifier that screens outputs. It does not decide whether something should be produced; it only shapes how strongly the conditioning is expressed in what is produced. Conflating the two leads to the mistaken belief that this dial governs content moderation.

For a security engineer, the practical value here is terminological precision: guidance strength and safety filtering are separate concerns living in different parts of a generative system. Any effects of guidance settings on specific behaviors should be taken from the cited source rather than inferred.
