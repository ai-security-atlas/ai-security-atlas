Embedding and reranking models turn inputs into vector representations, or score candidate items for relevance, to support retrieval and similarity workflows. Built on [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), an embedding model maps text or other content into a vector space where distance reflects semantic similarity, while a reranker takes a query and a candidate and produces a finer-grained relevance score. These models are the representational machinery beneath retrieval systems, and the atlas records them as a prerequisite for [corpora, vector indexes and rerankers](../nodes/system-index.md) and for [retrieval-augmented generation](../nodes/system-rag.md).

## Why it matters for security analysis

Their role is representation and scoring, not open-ended generation, so security analysis should attach to how those vectors and scores are used downstream: what content gets indexed, how similarity drives retrieval, and how retrieved items influence a larger system. A model that ranks or embeds does not present the token-by-token generation surface of a language model, and techniques scoped to generation should not be assumed to apply. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own, so applicable scope is best reasoned from the retrieval pipeline it feeds and from model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md). Design and empirical specifics are deferred to the cited literature.
