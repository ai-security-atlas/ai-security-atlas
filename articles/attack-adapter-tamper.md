This technique distributes or applies an attacker-controlled adaptation artifact so that a model's behavior changes once the artifact is loaded. Its objective is to [establish persistent attacker influence](../nodes/goal-persistence.md) and to [violate intended behavioral constraints](../nodes/goal-behavior.md): because the change is carried in a reusable weight-delta rather than a single prompt, altered behavior can survive across sessions.

Conceptually the technique builds on the mechanics of parameter-efficient adaptation such as [LoRA](../nodes/adapt-lora.md) and on the channels through which such artifacts circulate, including [model registries and distribution](../nodes/system-registry.md). The attacker-controlled input is the adapter itself, or a substituted version of an expected one; the victim-controlled state is the base model and the loading pipeline that trusts whatever adapter it is handed. Because adapters are small and composable, a consumer may apply one without the scrutiny a full checkpoint would receive.

The theoretical objective — behavioral change under attacker control — should be separated from any measured effect on a particular model. An adapter's file format alone does not establish a backdoor; that requires artifact-specific evidence.

Candidate mitigations reduce but do not remove risk. [Dataset and artifact provenance](../nodes/defense-provenance.md) can help establish where an adapter originated, and [safety and utility regression testing](../nodes/defense-regression.md) may surface behavioral drift introduced by adaptation. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack sits in the training-and-adaptation-abuse family. For empirical specifics — which models, and how strongly behavior shifts — defer to the cited sources.
