This family groups techniques that adversarially modify a model's internals or its runtime activations. The unifying assumption is white-box access: the attacker can read or change weights, or intervene on the hidden representations a model computes, rather than working only through prompts or training data.

Members target different internal handles. [Adversarial refusal-direction modification](../nodes/attack-refusal-ablation.md) removes or weakens the internal direction that mediates refusals, and [Adversarial activation steering](../nodes/attack-steering-misuse.md) pushes activations toward attacker-chosen behavior at runtime. [Unauthorized factual model editing](../nodes/attack-factual-edit.md) rewrites specific stored associations, while [Behavioral tampering through merges or conversion](../nodes/attack-merge-tamper.md) can hide changes inside model-merging or format-conversion steps.

These route toward *Violate intended behavioral constraints*, *Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions*, and *Establish persistent attacker influence*. The family sits close to the poisoning and training-abuse families, but it is distinguished by mechanism: interpretability- and editing-derived interventions applied to a model that already exists, rather than corruption introduced through the learning process. Several of these methods originate as legitimate research or model-editing tools, which is why the grouping frames them as capabilities that can be misused. Use it to navigate threats that presuppose direct access to representations or weights.
