This family groups techniques that target measurement and oversight rather than a model's primary task. The shared idea is that many AI pipelines rely on automated scoring—graders, judges, and benchmarks—to decide what is safe, correct, or ready to ship. If an attacker can distort those signals, they may either evade a specific oversight control or make a system appear better than it is.

Two members illustrate the range. [Automated judge or grader manipulation](../nodes/attack-judge-manipulation.md) targets the model-based or automated evaluator itself, shaping inputs so the judge returns a favorable verdict. [Benchmark or evaluation contamination](../nodes/attack-eval-contamination.md) corrupts the measurement process upstream, for example by influencing the data a benchmark draws on so that reported results no longer reflect genuine capability or safety.

These route toward *Evade security controls or accountability* and, for contamination, *Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions*. What sets this family apart from the evasion or prompt families is the target: the control plane of assessment, not the deployed model's live decisions. Treat it as a reminder that evaluation is itself an attack surface—compromised measurement can quietly license unsafe behavior elsewhere. The grouping is an orientation aid, and its members often depend on techniques catalogued in other families to deliver their manipulations.
