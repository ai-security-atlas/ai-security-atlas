Model inversion and attribute inference aim to [obtain protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md) by deducing sensitive attributes or input-related properties from a model's behavior combined with auxiliary knowledge the attacker already holds. The subject of inference is typically an individual or record represented in the model, not the model's own parameters.

Conceptually the technique reads a model's [logits, probabilities and observable outputs](../nodes/concept-logits.md) and correlates them with known features to narrow down an unknown attribute. The attacker-controlled input is the set of probing queries and any side information; the victim-controlled state is the trained model and whatever it has internalized about the population. It applies to both [classical predictive ML](../nodes/arch-classical.md) and [neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md).

A learner should keep three outcomes separate, because they are frequently conflated: inferring an attribute, reconstructing a class prototype, and extracting an exact training example are distinct results with different evidentiary bars. This technique also differs from [textual inversion](../nodes/adapt-textual-inversion.md), which learns an embedding to represent a concept rather than inferring a private attribute.

Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and [privacy verification and baselines](../nodes/eval-privacy.md) — the last being essential, since an inference that merely matches population base rates demonstrates little. Whether a given model leaks a specific attribute, and how reliably, is an empirical question; defer to the cited source.
