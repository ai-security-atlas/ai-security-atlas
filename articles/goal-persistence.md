This objective concerns attacks designed to *last*: making attacker-chosen behavior survive beyond a single session so it reappears on future triggers, across updates, or through ordinary retraining. Rather than a one-time effect, the attacker seeks a durable foothold in the model, its components or its memory that keeps paying off.

The techniques that route here plant that durability at different layers. In the weights and training signal, [pretraining data poisoning](../nodes/attack-pretrain-poison.md), [instruction-tuning data poisoning](../nodes/attack-sft-poison.md), [trigger-conditioned backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md), [persistent conditional backdoors](../nodes/attack-sleeper.md), diffusion backdoors and [unauthorized factual editing](../nodes/attack-factual-edit.md) embed behavior that can lie dormant until a trigger appears. In the component and supply layer, malicious adapters, [artifact or configuration substitution](../nodes/attack-artifact-substitution.md) and behavioral tampering through merges or conversion carry influence into what gets deployed. In agent and runtime state, [persistent agent-memory poisoning](../nodes/attack-memory-poison.md), tool-definition changes after approval and inter-agent trust manipulation let effects outlive the turn that introduced them.

What unifies them is the *time horizon*: the behavior is meant to persist, often quietly, past the moment of compromise. This overlaps with objectives that manipulate decisions or violate constraints, but here the defining property is survival across sessions and updates. For each mechanism's dormancy and triggering, see the linked techniques.
