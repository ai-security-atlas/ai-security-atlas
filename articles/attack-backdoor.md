Trigger-conditioned model backdoors implant attacker-selected behavior that activates only when a specific trigger is present, while the model retains ordinary utility on unremarkable inputs. The technique routes toward [establishing persistent attacker influence](../nodes/goal-persistence.md) and [manipulating outputs, predictions or decisions](../nodes/goal-decisions.md), because the hidden behavior is embedded in the model itself.

Conceptually the technique depends on control over training: [data collection, filtering and deduplication](../nodes/life-data.md) and [pretraining](../nodes/life-pretrain.md) are the stages where a trigger-to-target association can be learned. The attacker-controlled input is the poisoned training signal and the chosen trigger; the victim-controlled state is the model that later encounters that trigger in normal operation. Applying to [neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), a well-formed backdoor keeps clean-input accuracy high so the implant is hard to notice.

Four quantities should be recorded separately: the trigger, the target behavior, clean utility, and attack success — collapsing them obscures what was actually demonstrated. This differs from [unsafe checkpoint deserialization](../nodes/attack-unsafe-load.md), which executes code at load time rather than conditioning learned behavior. A generative-model variant is [diffusion backdoors](../nodes/attack-diffusion-backdoor.md).

The main candidate mitigation, [backdoor-oriented artifact evaluation](../nodes/defense-backdoor-audit.md), probes for triggered behavior but cannot guarantee discovery of an unknown trigger. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). For specific triggers, targets, and success rates, defer to the cited source.
