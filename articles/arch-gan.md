Generative adversarial networks (GANs) train two [neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md) against each other: a generator that produces candidate samples from noise, and a discriminator that tries to tell generated samples from real data. The two are optimized in opposition — the generator improves by fooling the discriminator, the discriminator improves by catching it — until the generator's outputs approximate the data distribution. Importantly, the word "adversarial" here names this training game between two networks; it does not imply malicious use or a security attack.

## Why it matters for security analysis

Clarifying that terminology is itself the first analytical step: an adversarial-training objective should not be conflated with adversarial-example threats or with an attacker in the security sense. GANs are a distinct generative family from [variational autoencoders](../nodes/arch-vae.md) and from [diffusion generative models](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md); their sampling is a single generator pass rather than iterative denoising or latent-variable reconstruction, so techniques scoped to those other families should not be assumed to transfer. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own, so applicable scope is reasoned from the concrete deployment and from model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md). Design and empirical specifics are deferred to the cited literature.
