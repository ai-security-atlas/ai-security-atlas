This family groups techniques that reinterpret or override a model's instructions through inputs the model can see. The shared mechanism is that language models act on their whole context without a firm boundary between trusted instructions and untrusted content, so an attacker who controls part of that context may steer behavior.

Members differ in delivery and goal. [Direct prompt injection](../nodes/attack-direct-injection.md) supplies adversarial instructions through the user channel, while [Indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md) hides them in content the model later ingests. [Jailbreaking and refusal bypass](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md) and [Many-shot context attacks](../nodes/attack-many-shot.md) aim to relax behavioral constraints; [Gradient-guided adversarial suffixes](../nodes/attack-gcg.md) and Transfer of adversarial prompts do so with optimized, portable strings. Instructions carried through multimodal content and System or developer prompt disclosure round out the set.

These route toward *Violate intended behavioral constraints*, *Cause unauthorized actions or privilege use*, *Manipulate outputs, predictions or decisions*, and *Obtain protected information*. The atlas explicitly separates indirect injection from jailbreaking: injection is about *whose* instructions the model follows—an untrusted source—whereas jailbreaking is about getting a model to *ignore its own constraints*. The two often combine, but the distinction guides where a defense should sit. Treat the grouping as navigation, not a strict taxonomy.
