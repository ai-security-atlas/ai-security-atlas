Representation engineering is a methodological umbrella for analyzing and modifying a model's internal representations rather than only its inputs and outputs. The idea is that concepts and behaviors are encoded in the model's intermediate activations, and that these can be read out, interpreted and in some cases adjusted. A top-down formulation appears in *Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency*.

The approach requires access to and reasoning about a model's [weights, parameters and activations](../nodes/concept-parameters.md), since representations live in those internal states. It is neutral by design: it names a way of working with internals, not a particular intervention or intent.

Several more specific techniques build on it. [Activation steering](../nodes/adapt-steering.md) uses representation-level directions to change runtime behavior, and [refusal-direction ablation / abliteration](../nodes/adapt-abliteration.md) identifies and suppresses a direction tied to refusal. Because the same read-and-modify capability serves both interpretability and behavior change, representation engineering is relevant to transparency research and to safety analysis, while its downstream uses may be benign or adversarial depending on the goal. Concrete methods for extracting and validating directions are left to the cited source.
