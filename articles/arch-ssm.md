State-space and hybrid sequence models process sequences by maintaining and updating a recurrent internal state rather than by attending over all positions at once. Built on [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), selective state-space designs propagate information along the sequence with computation that scales roughly linearly in length, offering an alternative to attention. Hybrid variants combine state-space layers with attention blocks, so a given model may be partly one and partly the other. The atlas presents these as an alternative or hybrid form of sequence computation.

## Why it matters for security analysis

The central caution for analysis is that architecture-specific manipulations do not transfer automatically. Techniques that target the internals of a transformer — attention patterns, residual-stream activations, or edits tied to that block structure — are not guaranteed to apply to a state-space model, whose sequence mixing works through recurrent state instead. Even in hybrids, the applicable surface depends on which layers do the relevant work. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own, so applicable scope should be reasoned from the concrete design — how much is state-space versus attention — and from model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), distinct from assumptions carried over from [Transformers](../nodes/arch-transformer.md). Empirical specifics are deferred to the cited literature.
