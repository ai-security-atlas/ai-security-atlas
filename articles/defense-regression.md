Safety and utility regression testing re-evaluates a model whenever it changes — after fine-tuning, adapter application, merges, format conversion, or configuration edits — against held-out safety and capability tests, so that a change that quietly weakens constraints is caught before deployment. It depends on [evaluation data and benchmark governance](../nodes/life-evaluate.md): maintained, held-out test sets whose integrity is preserved so results reflect genuine behavior rather than data the model has seen.

As a mitigation it is conditional. It can surface [adversarial fine-tuning to alter constraints](../nodes/attack-safety-finetune.md), [behavioral tampering through merges or conversion](../nodes/attack-merge-tamper.md), and [malicious adapters or adapter substitution](../nodes/attack-adapter-tamper.md) by detecting regressions in safety behavior between a trusted baseline and a modified artifact. Its coverage is only as good as the tests: behaviors not represented in the suite, or triggers designed to stay dormant under evaluation, can pass. Cited work shows that even benign fine-tuning can erode safety, which is why re-testing on change is warranted, but tests can be gamed or simply incomplete.

Regression testing is detective and complements provenance and load-time verification, which address how tampering enters. Consult the cited fine-tuning and red-teaming evaluation work for methodology and known limits.
