Adversarial training improves a model's resistance to input perturbations by including adversarially perturbed examples during training and evaluating against an explicit perturbation set and adaptive attacks. It is tied to [iterative projected adversarial optimization](../nodes/attack-pgd.md): strong iterative attacks are used both to generate training examples and to test the result, on the premise that robustness must be measured against the strongest available adversary rather than weak or fixed ones.

As a mitigation it is conditional and bounded by the threat model it targets. It can reduce susceptibility to [gradient-based adversarial perturbations / FGSM](../nodes/attack-fgsm.md) and [iterative projected adversarial optimization](../nodes/attack-pgd.md) within the perturbation set it was trained and evaluated against. Outside that set — different norms, larger budgets, or unanticipated attack types — protection may not transfer, and robustness typically comes at a cost to clean accuracy and training compute. Apparent robustness measured only against weak attacks can also overstate real resistance, which is why adaptive evaluation is emphasized.

Its guarantees are empirical and specific to the stated perturbation model, not general immunity to adversarial inputs. Consult the cited work on models resistant to adversarial attacks for the training and evaluation methodology.
