Diffusion models generate by learning to reverse a gradual corruption process: during training, data is progressively mixed with random noise across a series of timesteps, and the model learns to predict and remove that noise. Two ingredients define this setup. The *noise schedule* specifies how much noise is added at each timestep — the trajectory from clean data to near-pure noise. The *denoising objective* is what the model is trained to predict at each step, such as the added noise itself, so that repeated denoising can turn random noise into a sample. This concept builds on [diffusion generative models](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md).

The reason to isolate these choices is that they are diffusion-*specific*: the noise parameterization and timestep structure are what distinguish interventions aimed at diffusion systems from those aimed at other architectures. An operation defined in terms of timesteps or noise levels only makes sense against a model built this way.

Noise schedules and objectives are a prerequisite for [diffusion and flow samplers](../nodes/concept-samplers.md), which run the reverse process at inference, and for [diffusion backdoors / BadDiffusion](../nodes/attack-diffusion-backdoor.md), which manipulate the training-time process. Understanding where the noise process sits is groundwork for those topics; their specifics, including any demonstrated behaviors, are covered by the cited sources.
