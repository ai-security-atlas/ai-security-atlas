Full-parameter fine-tuning updates most or all of a model's weights to adapt it to a new objective, domain or dataset. Unlike methods that freeze the base and train only a small set of added parameters, this approach lets the entire network move, which gives it the most capacity to change behavior at the cost of more compute, memory and data.

The method is a direct application of [supervised fine-tuning](../nodes/life-sft.md): a labeled dataset defines the target behavior, and the optimizer adjusts the weights to fit it. It is a standard, neutral adaptation step used to specialize general-purpose models for particular tasks or styles.

Because it can reshape a model broadly, full fine-tuning is also the mechanism behind [adversarial fine-tuning to alter constraints](../nodes/attack-safety-finetune.md), where training is directed at weakening or removing safety behavior. As noted in *Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety, Even When Users Do Not Intend To!*, changes to safety-relevant behavior can arise even without malicious intent, which is why the outcome depends on the data and objective rather than the procedure. The empirical extent of such effects is documented in the cited source.
