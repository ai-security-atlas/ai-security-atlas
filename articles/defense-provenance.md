Dataset and artifact provenance records the identities, immutable revisions, transformations, and approval history behind the data and models an organization uses. It spans [data collection, filtering and deduplication](../nodes/life-data.md) and [model registries and distribution](../nodes/system-registry.md), attaching a verifiable trail to each dataset, checkpoint, and adapter so that what is deployed can be traced to a known, approved source rather than accepted on trust.

As a mitigation it is conditional. It can reduce [artifact, adapter or configuration substitution](../nodes/attack-artifact-substitution.md) and [malicious adapters or adapter substitution](../nodes/attack-adapter-tamper.md) by making unauthorized swaps detectable against recorded revisions, and it supports investigation of [pretraining data poisoning](../nodes/attack-pretrain-poison.md) by identifying which data entered a model. Provenance is largely detective: it records and verifies but does not by itself block tampering. Residual risk includes compromise of the provenance system itself, poisoned data that was legitimately recorded, and gaps where transformations are untracked. Recording an artifact's origin says nothing about whether that origin was trustworthy.

It is most effective combined with verification at load and deploy time and with least-privilege pipelines. The cited taxonomy and threat knowledge base describe the supply-chain integrity practices provenance underpins.
