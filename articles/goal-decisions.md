This objective covers attacks that bend an AI system's *result* in the attacker's favor — a classification, a ranking, a generated artifact, or a downstream decision that acts on the model's output. Unlike attacks that seek forbidden behavior or stolen data, the payoff here is a specific outcome: the spam scored as safe, the attacker's document ranked first, the answer that quietly favors one party.

The techniques that route here reach the decision from many angles. Inference-time perturbations such as [gradient-based adversarial perturbations / FGSM](../nodes/attack-fgsm.md), [iterative projected optimization](../nodes/attack-pgd.md) and [physical-world adversarial examples](../nodes/attack-physical.md) alter inputs to flip outputs. Training-time influence — [pretraining data poisoning](../nodes/attack-pretrain-poison.md), preference-signal manipulation, [trigger-conditioned backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md), diffusion backdoors, model merging tampering and [unauthorized factual editing](../nodes/attack-factual-edit.md) — biases the model before it ever runs. In retrieval and agent settings, [RAG knowledge-base poisoning](../nodes/attack-rag-poison.md), [retrieval and ranking manipulation](../nodes/attack-retrieval-rank.md), [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md), multimodal injection, evaluation contamination and citation laundering steer the context or the evidence a decision rests on.

What unifies them is a targeted change to the answer rather than to the system's permissions or its rules. Treat this as "was the outcome tilted," and follow the individual techniques for how each tilt is achieved.
