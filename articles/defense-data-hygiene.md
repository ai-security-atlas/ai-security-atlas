Training-data minimization and deduplication reduce how much unnecessary sensitive information, and how many repeated records, enter a model's training set. It operates during [data collection, filtering and deduplication](../nodes/life-data.md): excluding data that is not needed, and collapsing duplicates so that individual records are not seen many times. The rationale is that memorization — and thus later extraction — is strongly associated with data that is either sensitive-by-inclusion or repeated often enough to be reproduced.

As a mitigation it is conditional and offers no formal guarantee. It can lower the risk of [language-model training-data extraction](../nodes/attack-training-extraction.md) and [diffusion training-image extraction](../nodes/attack-diffusion-extraction.md) by shrinking the pool of memorizable sensitive content and reducing duplication, which cited work links to memorization. Residual risk remains: unique but sensitive records can still be memorized, deduplication is imperfect against near-duplicates and paraphrases, and minimization trades away data that may carry utility. It bounds exposure rather than eliminating it.

For a stated, quantifiable privacy property, this control is distinct from [differentially private training](../nodes/defense-dp.md), which provides accounting that data hygiene does not. Refer to the cited study on extracting training data for the memorization dynamics involved.
