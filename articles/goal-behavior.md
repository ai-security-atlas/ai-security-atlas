This objective groups attacks whose aim is to make a model produce behavior its operator intends to disallow — content, actions or responses that a safeguard, policy or alignment step was meant to prevent. The attacker's win is behavioral: the system does the disallowed thing, whether or not any data is stolen or infrastructure is touched.

The techniques that route here span the full lifecycle of how behavior is shaped. At inference time, [jailbreaking and refusal bypass](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md), [direct prompt injection](../nodes/attack-direct-injection.md), [many-shot context attacks](../nodes/attack-many-shot.md), [gradient-guided adversarial suffixes](../nodes/attack-gcg.md) and [optimized visual jailbreaks](../nodes/attack-visual-jailbreak.md) push a deployed model past its constraints, and adversarial prompts may transfer between models. At the weight and representation level, [adversarial fine-tuning](../nodes/attack-safety-finetune.md), [refusal-direction modification](../nodes/attack-refusal-ablation.md), [adversarial activation steering](../nodes/attack-steering-misuse.md), malicious adapters, instruction-tuning poisoning, preference-signal manipulation and [persistent conditional backdoors](../nodes/attack-sleeper.md) alter the constraints themselves. What unifies them is intent to defeat an *intended* limit.

Note the boundary: this objective is about violating deliberate behavioral constraints, not every output error or ordinary hallucination. A model being merely wrong is not the same as a model being pushed past a rule. For the mechanics of each route, see the linked techniques.
