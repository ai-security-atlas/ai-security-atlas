An explicit threat model states the assumptions under which an attack or defense is evaluated: the adversary's goals, their capabilities and access, what prior knowledge they hold, their query or compute budgets, the authority granted to the system under attack, and which target revisions are in scope. Making these explicit is what turns a result into something comparable and interpretable, because the same technique can look devastating or ineffective depending on whether the attacker is assumed to have white-box access, black-box queries, or a fixed budget.

This framing underlies evaluation across the atlas, from prompt-level attacks like [jailbreaking and refusal bypass](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md) and [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md) to model-level ones like [black-box functional model extraction](../nodes/attack-model-imitation.md) and supply-chain ones like [malicious dependencies or repository code](../nodes/attack-dependency.md). Each is only meaningful once the assumed adversary is fixed.

A common pitfall is an unstated or shifting threat model, where a defense is tested against a weaker adversary than the one it claims to stop, or a result generated under white-box access is presented as a black-box risk. Conclusions are scope-limited to the stated model: strong results under one set of assumptions are not proof of safety under a stronger adversary, more access, or a larger budget. A clear threat model also makes clear what was not tested. Defer to the cited taxonomy for standardized threat-model terminology.
