This entry covers the misuse of a model's ordinary generation, analysis, or automation capabilities for harmful purposes — routing toward the objective of [using AI to amplify harmful activity](../nodes/goal-misuse.md). What distinguishes it from most other techniques here is that it need not break or subvert the model at all: the capability is used more or less as intended, but toward a harmful end.

Conceptually the technique treats the model as a force multiplier. The attacker supplies a task; the model's fluent generation across [language](../nodes/arch-decoder.md) or [diffusion generative](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) modalities lowers the effort, skill, or time a harmful activity would otherwise require. Because no policy override is strictly necessary, this is best understood as a risk category rather than a single exploit, and it overlaps with — but is broader than — jailbreaking, which specifically targets behavioral constraints.

Consistent with responsible study, the mechanism is described at the level of risk rather than as any operational payload or unauthorized target automation.

The candidate mitigation noted here is [responsible disclosure and safe research](../nodes/defense-disclosure.md), a process control that governs how such risks are investigated and reported rather than a runtime filter. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). This attack sits in the capability-misuse family. For scoped assessments of where amplification is meaningful, defer to the cited sources.
