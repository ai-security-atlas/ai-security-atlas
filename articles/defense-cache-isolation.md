Cache and session isolation partitions reusable serving state so that data belonging to one user, tenant, or session cannot leak into another's requests or be inferred through shared infrastructure. It concerns [KV caches and prefix reuse](../nodes/concept-kv-cache.md), where caching prompt prefixes and intermediate state across requests improves performance but can create cross-request coupling. The control keeps such state scoped to its owner and measures information-sharing signals — including timing — under realistic conditions rather than assuming isolation holds.

As a mitigation it is conditional. It can reduce [cross-session or cross-tenant disclosure](../nodes/attack-session-leak.md) by preventing one principal's cached content from surfacing in another's context, and it can narrow [prompt-cache timing inference](../nodes/attack-cache-sidechannel.md) by removing or masking the timing differences that reveal whether a prefix was cached. Residual risk remains: strict per-principal partitioning reduces cache hit rates and raises cost, so pressure to share caches for efficiency reopens the channel, and side channels beyond the ones measured may persist. An adaptive attacker can probe for whatever sharing the deployment still permits.

Because the trade-off is between performance and leakage, isolation decisions are best validated empirically. Consult the cited work on auditing prompt caching for measurement techniques and observed leakage.
