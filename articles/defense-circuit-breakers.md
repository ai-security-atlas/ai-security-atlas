Representation rerouting and activation circuit breakers monitor or redirect a model's internal activations during the forward pass so that harmful generation trajectories or backdoor triggers are interrupted before tokens are emitted. The control builds directly on [representation engineering](../nodes/adapt-representation.md) and the [residual stream](../nodes/concept-residual.md) of [transformer](../nodes/arch-transformer.md) and [decoder-only language models](../nodes/arch-decoder.md).

Unlike external input/output classifiers that inspect surface text, representation-level defenses act on intermediate hidden states. *Circuit Breakers* (Zou et al., NeurIPS 2024) train LoRA or weight updates that map internal representations associated with harmful outputs toward orthogonal or halting states whenever those representations arise, whereas linear *defection probes* (*Simple Probes Can Catch Sleeper Agents*) score residual-stream activations to detect when a model is about to defect or trigger a hidden behavior.

Because internal representations of harmful generation or deceptive defection converge even when the surface input uses an [optimized adversarial suffix](../nodes/attack-gcg.md), a [visual jailbreak](../nodes/attack-visual-jailbreak.md), [chain-of-thought hijacking](../nodes/attack-cot-hijack.md), [constrained-decoding bypass](../nodes/attack-constrained-decoding-bypass.md), or a [conditional sleeper backdoor](../nodes/attack-sleeper.md), circuit breakers and activation probes provide defense-in-depth where prompt-level refusal training fails. However, they require white-box serving access and careful threshold calibration to avoid false-positive interruptions on benign edge cases.
