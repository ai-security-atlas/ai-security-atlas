Convolutional networks extract features by sliding learned filters across spatially structured inputs, most commonly images. Built on [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), they exploit locality and weight sharing: early layers respond to simple patterns like edges, while deeper layers compose these into higher-level features, often with pooling that builds tolerance to small shifts. This spatial inductive bias makes them a longstanding backbone for vision tasks, and the atlas notes their role inside [U-Net-style diffusion backbones](../nodes/arch-unet.md).

## Why it matters for security analysis

Because inputs are continuous images, the relevant threat surface centers on perturbations to pixels. The atlas marks [physical-world adversarial examples](../nodes/attack-physical.md) as relevant to this scope — perturbations that survive printing, lighting, and camera capture rather than existing only as digital deltas. This indicates applicability, not a demonstrated weakness in any particular model. Convolutional models are also subject to the broader, model-level concerns inherited via [Neural networks](../nodes/arch-neural.md), including supply-chain backdoors and evasion. Analysis should stay grounded in the specific task: what the network classifies or detects, how its outputs drive decisions, and whether an adversary can control inputs in the deployment environment. Empirical thresholds and attack conditions are deferred to the cited literature.
