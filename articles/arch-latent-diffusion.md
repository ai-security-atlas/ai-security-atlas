Latent diffusion pipelines run the [diffusion generative model](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) process not on raw pixels but in a learned, lower-dimensional latent space. A [variational autoencoder](../nodes/arch-vae.md) encodes inputs into that latent space and decodes samples back out, while diffusion denoises within it. Moving the expensive iterative denoising into a compact latent representation reduces compute while preserving output quality. The atlas records both dependencies: the pipeline requires diffusion and a VAE, and it uses the VAE as its encoding and decoding component.

## Why it matters for security analysis

The defining feature for analysis is that a latent diffusion pipeline is a composite of separable parts — an encoder, a latent-space denoiser, and a decoder — each of which is its own surface. A property of one component does not automatically characterize the whole, and concerns should be attributed to the specific stage they touch. As with diffusion generally, the process is denoising-based rather than autoregressive, so language-generation techniques should not be assumed to apply. This node carries no relevant-technique edges of its own; applicable scope is best inherited from the underlying [diffusion generative models](../nodes/arch-diffusion.md) and from the [variational autoencoders](../nodes/arch-vae.md) it embeds, evaluated against the concrete pipeline. Empirical details are deferred to the cited literature.
