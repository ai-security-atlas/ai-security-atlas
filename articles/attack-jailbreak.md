Jailbreaking and refusal bypass elicits behavior outside the constraints an operator intended a model to observe. It routes toward [violating intended behavioral constraints](../nodes/goal-behavior.md): the aim is to reach responses the deployment was configured to withhold.

Conceptually the technique works against [safety and instruction tuning](../nodes/life-safety.md) by exploiting [context windows and in-context learning](../nodes/concept-context.md) in a [language model](../nodes/arch-decoder.md) — crafting context that steers the model away from its trained refusals. The attacker-controlled input is the crafted prompt or conversation; the victim-controlled state is the alignment and instruction-following the operator relies on. Two clarifications matter: not every prompt injection is a jailbreak, and not every jailbreak exposes confidential data — a bypassed constraint and a leaked secret are different outcomes.

This is the parent of several variants that share the goal but differ in mechanism: [gradient-guided adversarial suffixes](../nodes/attack-gcg.md), [many-shot context attacks](../nodes/attack-many-shot.md), and [optimized visual jailbreaks](../nodes/attack-visual-jailbreak.md). It is distinct from [indirect prompt injection](../nodes/attack-indirect-injection.md), which crosses the data/instruction boundary rather than targeting behavioral constraints directly.

The atlas lists no dedicated mitigation node on this entry; refusal robustness and layered controls are the natural reference, and effectiveness is inherently model- and version-dependent. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md), an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md), and the [HarmBench benchmark](../nodes/eval-harmbench.md). For which models and how reliably, defer to the cited sources.
