Memorization is when a model retains information specific to individual training examples, such that particular records can be reproduced or detected, rather than only learning the general patterns of its data. This is distinct from *generalization*, the intended outcome, where a model captures the underlying distribution and performs well on inputs it never saw. The two are not opposites in practice — a model can generalize well and still memorize some examples — but they are conceptually separate, and conflating them obscures a real privacy surface. *Duplication* is closely related: examples repeated many times in the training data tend to be more prone to memorization.

This concept builds on [weights, parameters and activations](../nodes/concept-parameters.md), because memorized content, when present, is stored implicitly in the learned parameters rather than in any explicit database.

The security significance is that memorization is what makes certain data-exposure attacks conceivable. It is a prerequisite for [language-model training-data extraction](../nodes/attack-training-extraction.md), [diffusion training-image extraction](../nodes/attack-diffusion-extraction.md), and [membership inference](../nodes/attack-membership.md), each of which turns retained training-specific information into a leak. The degree to which any given model memorizes, and how readily that content can be surfaced, is an empirical matter addressed by the cited studies rather than something to assume.
