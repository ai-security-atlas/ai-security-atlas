Data collection, filtering and deduplication is the foundational lifecycle stage where raw corpora are gathered, cleaned, filtered and deduplicated before any training objective is applied. Its preprocessing choices — what is included, how duplicates are removed, and how sources are documented — shape a model's later privacy, integrity and evaluation reliability. Because no earlier stage feeds it, the trust placed in data sources here propagates forward: [pretraining](../nodes/life-pretrain.md) and downstream evaluation both depend on it.

## Security-relevant scope

Several concerns are relevant to this stage rather than proven at it. Dataset lineage bears on whether later [training-data minimization and deduplication](../nodes/defense-data-hygiene.md) and [dataset and artifact provenance](../nodes/defense-provenance.md) practices can be applied. It is also the point at which data-centric manipulation such as [pretraining data poisoning](../nodes/attack-pretrain-poison.md) or [trigger-conditioned model backdoors](../nodes/attack-backdoor.md) would, if present, be introduced. Deduplication additionally affects how memorization and extraction risk are later analyzed.

Keeping training data cleanly separated from evaluation material is also what makes [evaluation and benchmark governance](../nodes/life-evaluate.md) meaningful. The cited work on extracting training data and the NIST adversarial-ML taxonomy give empirical and definitional grounding for these exposures; treat the attack references as scope, not as demonstrated compromise of any particular dataset.
