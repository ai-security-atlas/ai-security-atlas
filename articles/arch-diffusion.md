Diffusion generative models learn to produce data by reversing a gradual noising process. During training, samples are progressively corrupted toward noise, and a network learns to denoise; at generation time, the model starts from noise and iteratively denoises into a sample. Crucially, this describes a family of objectives and sampling procedures, not a single backbone: the same denoising principle can be paired with different networks and spaces, which the atlas reflects through downstream nodes such as [U-Net-style diffusion backbones](../nodes/arch-unet.md), [diffusion transformers](../nodes/arch-dit.md), [latent diffusion pipelines](../nodes/arch-latent-diffusion.md), [noise schedules and denoising objectives](../nodes/concept-noise.md), and [classifier-free guidance](../nodes/concept-guidance.md).

## Why it matters for security analysis

Diffusion is iterative and denoising-based, not autoregressive; techniques scoped to token-by-token language generation should not be assumed to carry over. The concerns relevant here follow from training on image data and from the generative capability itself: the atlas marks [diffusion backdoors / BadDiffusion](../nodes/attack-diffusion-backdoor.md), [diffusion training-image extraction](../nodes/attack-diffusion-extraction.md), and [AI-assisted harmful activity](../nodes/attack-capability-abuse.md) as relevant to this scope. These indicate applicability, not demonstrated weakness in any specific system. Because diffusion is realized through varied backbones and pipelines, analysis should attach to the concrete configuration in use — the network, the space it operates in, and its adaptation methods. Empirical specifics are deferred to the cited literature.
