Indirect prompt injection places attacker instructions inside lower-trust documents or tool content that an application later ingests and treats as authority. It routes toward [causing unauthorized actions or privilege use](../nodes/goal-actions.md), [manipulating outputs, predictions or decisions](../nodes/goal-decisions.md), and [obtaining protected information](../nodes/goal-information.md).

Conceptually the technique exploits [context windows and in-context learning](../nodes/concept-context.md) together with [trust boundaries and authorization](../nodes/concept-boundaries.md): when retrieved or tool-supplied content shares the context with genuine instructions, embedded commands may be followed. The attacker-controlled input is the seeded external content; the victim-controlled state is the [retrieval-augmented generation](../nodes/system-rag.md) or [tool-using agent](../nodes/system-agent.md) pipeline that fetches it. A necessary condition — and the reason it is not automatic — is that the content must actually be ingested and reach a privileged workflow.

It differs from [jailbreaking](../nodes/attack-jailbreak.md), whose target is behavioral constraints rather than the data/instruction boundary. It can enable [confused-deputy abuse](../nodes/attack-confused-deputy.md), may be enabled by [agent-memory](../nodes/attack-memory-poison.md) or [RAG-corpus](../nodes/attack-rag-poison.md) poisoning, and has channel-specific variants in [tool-description poisoning](../nodes/attack-tool-description.md) and [tool-output injection](../nodes/attack-tool-output.md).

The candidate mitigation is to [separate data from authority](../nodes/defense-boundaries.md) so ingested content is not treated as commands; this reduces but does not eliminate risk. Evaluation is framed through [attack success and severity](../nodes/eval-asr.md) under an [explicit threat model](../nodes/eval-threat-model.md). For demonstrated impact, defer to the cited source.
